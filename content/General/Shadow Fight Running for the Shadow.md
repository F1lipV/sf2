**Shadow Fight: Running for the Shadow** (Russian: _Shadow Fight. Бег за Тенью_) is a canon tie-in novel set in the Shadow Fight universe, written by Alex Foth and officially released by Nekki in collaboration with a local publisher, on July 25, 2025 in Russia. The novel explores the backstory of Shadow, known in the story by his real name — Kaginoko, and depicts his journey prior to becoming the legendary warrior recognized in the games. The novel serves as the first official narrative expansion of the Shadow Fight universe beyond the games, providing additional insight into the character’s origins and the world in which he lives. This novel can be purchased as a hard copy through select stores in Russia or as a soft copy through Google Books or select online Russian stores.

## Official Description\[]

_Since childhood, Kaginoko trained diligently under experienced teachers to master the art of combat and become the world's greatest warrior. He had never lost a single tournament, and his list of worthy opponents had dwindled. Then, on the advice of his assistant, Kaginoko set out in search of an opponent whose victory would bring him worldwide fame. According to ancient legends, at the edge of the world, in the mountains, lies a magical Gate inhabited by powerful demons — great warriors eager to enter the human world. However, Kaginoko believes in no demons, considering them a figment of his imagination, and agrees to the journey solely out of curiosity. The Guardians hold the pieces of the key that opens the Gate. Finding them and acquiring the pieces proves a daunting task. After a long journey, losing allies and fighting everyone who challenges him, Kaginoko reaches the desired location. And despite all the prohibitions of the ancient chroniclers, he opens the Gate._

## Overview\[]

Shadow Fight: Running for the Shadow follows Kaginoko, the warrior who would later become known as Shadow. Trained from childhood under skilled masters, he has defeated all challengers and seeks a worthy opponent to test his abilities. Legends tell of magical Gates at the edge of the world, guarded by powerful demonic warriors. Skeptical but curious, Kaginoko embarks on a journey to find the key fragments held by the Keepers, facing challenges and losing allies along the way. Ultimately, he reaches the Gates and opens them, setting the stage for his transformation into Shadow.

## Story\[]

### Prologue\[]

After being taken in by Master Carham following a street brawl at the age of nine, Kaginoko spends a year undergoing grueling training with a wooden katana. Carham, a renowned and muscular warrior who retired from major tournaments before Kaginoko was even born, seeks to transform the boy's raw athleticism into disciplined mastery. He teaches Kaginoko that a true duel is an art form defined by grace and mental fortitude, distinguishing it from a common street fight. As Kaginoko’s parents are away traveling for work, he dedicates himself entirely to these lessons, sleeping with his wooden weapon and dreaming of the day he will stand in the arena as the world's greatest fighter.

The climax of this period is Kaginoko’s first official duel against an older, taller bully nicknamed Gnus, who is notorious for his cruelty and skill with a wooden staff. During the fight, Kaginoko is initially overwhelmed by the physical reach of his opponent and the intense pressure of the bloodthirsty crowd. After taking several painful hits, he realizes that he cannot win through defense alone and launches a desperate, decisive counter-attack. The encounter ends violently as both fighters land simultaneous blows: Gnus breaks Kaginoko’s nose, but Kaginoko delivers a strike so powerful it shatters the bully's teeth and leaves him unconscious. Although the crowd is thrilled by the spectacle, Carham humbles his student by declaring the match a draw due to Kaginoko’s own injury. This bittersweet result leaves Kaginoko with a bruised ego but a hardened resolve to continue his training until his superiority is beyond question.

### Chapter 1\[]

After fifteen years of undefeated glory, the warrior Kaginoko finds himself paralyzed by a profound sense of existential boredom following a hollow victory over the massive but unskilled Hek the Nimble. Despite his fame and the wealth managed by his clever assistant Dtai, Kaginoko feels he has reached a dead end where no human opponent—be they great daimyo or legendary bodyguard—can offer the perfection he craves in a duel. His reputation has become so fearsome that a literal "invisible circle" of terror forms around him in public, isolating him from a world that no longer dares to look him in the eye.

Seeking to break this stagnation, Dtai proposes a journey to the legendary Gates of Shadows, a place dismissed by commoners as a myth but rumored to house supernatural demons and warriors of unmatched power. Kaginoko, enticed by the possibility of finding a true peer, leads an expedition into a desolate and mountainous wilderness where the roads eventually disappear. Guided by the scarred and apprehensive Tontor, the group pushes through superstitious fear and physical exhaustion, driven by Dtai’s secret research and Kaginoko’s relentless ambition to prove his invincibility against the divine or the damned.

In a secluded village, the travelers encounter Itaka, an elder who reveals that his lineage has guarded the truth of the Gates and a map to six hidden key fragments for generations. Itaka recounts the ancient legend of the Faceless One, who defeated the demons and scattered the keys to ensure the Gates remained sealed forever. Though the elder is initially resistant to aiding the warrior, Kaginoko’s refusal to use violence and his visible internal struggle earn Itaka's trust, leading the elder to provide his son Minoro as a guide to begin the hunt for the scattered fragments.

### Chapter 2\[]

Ten-year-old Kaginoko lives in a state of obsessive isolation, spending his days relentlessly refining his combat skills with a wooden katana while his peers, such as the baker’s son Frido and the slender Ekosi, engage in typical childhood games like skipping stones. This singular focus alienates him from his friends, who view his constant drills as a form of madness, yet Kaginoko privately envisions battling demonic creatures far more dangerous than any mortal beast. He rejects the simple futures his friends imagine for themselves, such as hunting or trading, believing that his destiny lies in becoming the world's greatest warrior and achieving a level of perfection that transcends ordinary human combat.

The boy's routine is shattered when his master, Carham, introduces a mysterious, white-haired high-ranking official known simply as [[Sensei (SF2)|Sensei]], who carries a prestigious tachi saber. Sensei immediately humbles Kaginoko's arrogance by easily sweeping his legs and flooring him, eventually pinning a tanto dagger to the boy's face to demonstrate the vast gap in their abilities. Carham explains that while he has provided Kaginoko with a foundation, Sensei has been brought in to teach the elusive mental discipline and "balance" required for a significant upcoming duel. Despite the boy’s mounting rage, Sensei remains stoic, humoring Kaginoko's outbursts while preparing him for the reality that a true warrior must master his internal grace.

Over a brutal three-week training regime, Kaginoko is pushed to his limits through complex coordination drills, such as juggling multiple balls while navigating obstacle courses and stairs. Sensei is revealed to be a former elite bodyguard for [[Prince]] who was exiled to the provinces after a [[Old Wounds (SF2SE)|failed assassination attempt]], leaving him a man of deep mystery and immense skill. Though the training is grueling and the Sensei's past remains largely hidden, he acknowledges Kaginoko’s raw talent by stating the boy has "no ceiling" to his potential. By the end of this period, Kaginoko’s resentment begins to transform into a begrudging respect as he realizes that the path to invincibility requires the seasoned wisdom only this veteran can provide.

### Chapter 3\[]

Venturing into the heart of a dense emerald forest, Kaginoko, Dtai, and their guides Tonto and Minoro find themselves on a narrow, winding trail that feels increasingly desolate and eerie. The group's morale is tested by Tonto’s deep-seated pessimism, born from a traumatic past as a galley slave, while Kaginoko remains focused on the legendary Gates as the only remaining cure for his existential boredom. Following Minoro’s lead, they discover a moss-covered hut in a clearing where no grass grows, and are met by a white-haired hermit who identifies himself as the Keeper. The man's appearance is deceptive; as he leaps into the clearing, the group is horrified to see that his legs have been replaced by a complex, gear-driven mechanical apparatus that hisses with steam and clicks with metallic precision.

The Keeper immediately challenges Kaginoko’s worthiness, forcing a brutal duel that pushes the warrior to his physical and mental limits. This mechanical veteran uses his metal limbs to deliver bone-crushing strikes and supernatural jumps, forcing Kaginoko to rely on the "balance" and reflexes he learned from Sensei during his youth. After a grueling exchange that leaves Kaginoko bloodied and exhausted, he manages to land a decisive blow on the Keeper's gear-work, causing the machine-man to collapse as his internal pistons fail. In his dying breath, the Keeper reveals he was once a seeker of the Gates who lost his humanity to become a guardian, warning Kaginoko that this path leads only to a transformation into something cold and unfeeling.

In the aftermath of the battle, the group recovers three rusted metal plates from a hidden chest—the very first fragment of the six keys needed to open the Gates of Shadows. While the rest of the party is shaken by the encounter with a "metal man" Kaginoko’s resolve only hardens as they retreat to a nearby tavern to recover from their wounds. He acknowledges the psychological toll the journey is taking on his men but refuses to turn back, instructing Dtai to begin recruiting mercenaries and specialists for the journey ahead before Kaginoko staring into the steam of a hot bath, haunted by the Keeper's warning but more determined than ever to face the remaining guardians and the demons that lie beyond.

### Chapter 4\[]

Under Sensei’s relentless guidance, Kaginoko’s youth becomes a blur of grueling drills and mental conditioning designed to erase hesitation. He masters the art of moving his body with automatic, lethal grace, navigating obstacle courses and practicing meditation to achieve a state where thought and action are one. This period of development is tested when a local bully named Gnus begins a savage assault on Kaginoko’s friend Frido. Despite Sensei’s cold command to remain a detached observer and treat the suffering as a "poisonous" distraction, Kaginoko chooses to intervene, soundly defeating the gang but earning a stern warning that such emotional attachments will eventually lower the "ceiling" of his potential.

The transition to manhood brings Kaginoko to his first elite tournament, where he is pit against a terrifying, tattooed giant known as the Bonebreaker. Though heavily outmatched in size and raw strength, the young warrior relies on the precise balance and explosive energy cultivated during his years of isolation. He dances around the giant’s bone-crushing strikes, waiting for a single lapse in the veteran’s rhythm before delivering a decisive, bloody counter-attack that leaves the arena in stunned silence. This victory serves as the official birth of his legend, proving that his technical perfection can overcome even the most savage physical force.

In the quiet aftermath of the duel, a wounded Kaginoko reflects on his path while recovering in a local tavern, realizing that his rise to glory has come at the cost of his humanity. While his master Carham views the win with pride, Sensei remains a shadow of doubt, dismissing the triumph as a stroke of luck and insisting that the boy has yet to truly master his internal spirit. Surrounded by the tools of his trade and the smell of herbal salves, Kaginoko accepts his role as a solitary seeker of strength, fully committing to the journey that will eventually lead him to the Gates of Shadows.

### Chapter 5\[]

Led by the veteran guide Tonto, Kaginoko’s newly recruited caravan of mercenaries and silent servants navigates a desolate, wind-swept plateau where the air is thick with a nauseating scent of decay. The group's morale wavers as they reach a massive, yawning abyss in the earth: an ancient mining complex where gray, dust-covered laborers toil alongside hissing steam-powered machinery and rhythmic, clanking gears. This industrial underworld is governed by the cold and calculating Hokori, the Mistress of the Mine, who views the world through the lens of absolute efficiency and profit. Kaginoko, Dtai, and Minoro are forced to descend into the lightless throat of the mine, surrendering their weapons as a show of compliance to enter Hokori's territory, where she reveals that her ancestors have mined the stone housing the legendary key fragments for generations.

The tension culminates in a dark, subterranean arena where Hokori demands a trial by combat to the first blood, pitting Kaginoko against her elite personal champion—a tattooed giant who wields a massive, chain-linked weapon that sweeps through the dust with bone-crushing force. Forced to fight without his usual gear and blinded by the swirling grit of the pit, Kaginoko is pushed to a psychological and physical breaking point. He eventually relies on the deep sensory training and "balance" taught by Sensei, allowing him to perceive the giant’s movements through the vibrations in the air and the sound of his opponent's irregular breathing. In a singular, explosive moment of focus, Kaginoko executes a lethal counter-strike that severs his opponent's wrist, leaving the champion broken and ending the duel in a stunned, heavy silence that echoes through the mine.

Following the bloody victory, Hokori honors the "law of the pit" and stops her guards from retaliating, though she warns Kaginoko that his obsession with the Gates of Shadows is a destructive poison that will eventually hollow out his spirit. She leads the group to a hidden chamber where she finally surrenders the second set of rusted metal fragments, completing another portion of the ancient key. The party makes a swift exit through a network of secret, damp tunnels, emerging back into the cold mountain air far from the reach of Hokori’s industrial empire. As they look toward the next location on the map, Kaginoko reflects on the increasing weight of the relics he carries, his resolve hardening despite the Keeper’s grim warnings about the loss of his own humanity.

### Chapter 6\[]

Years earlier, in a seaside town, the figure known as the Sensei survived in exile as a beggar and thief under the name Kojiki, hiding his lethal skills behind a ragged exterior. His mastery remained undiminished; he once neutralized a group of aggressive sailors with a wooden training sword, moving with such "balance" and speed that he left them stunned. During this period, he encountered the young Kaginoko, whose raw potential and lack of focus reminded him of his own past failures. Recognizing a talent with "no ceiling," Kojiki initiated a clandestine training regime—often involving blindfolded coordination drills—conducted in secret to avoid the notice of Kaginoko's primary master, Carham.

The secrecy of these lessons drives a wedge between Kaginoko and his community, forcing the boy to lie to Carham and abandon the childhood games of his friends to pursue a path they cannot understand. To test the boy's progress, Kojiki facilitates Kaginoko's entry into a high-stakes local tournament managed by the shrewd Yuslim the Fidgety, where Kaginoko must fight under a false identity. In the final, Kaginoko faces a nimble opponent nicknamed The Spider, utilizing the precise sensory awareness and "shadow" tactics Kojiki instilled in him. Kaginoko eventually secures a decisive victory, but the triumph is overshadowed by the realization that his friends, Frido and Zmei, now view him as a dangerous stranger.

Tense negotiation between Kojiki and Yuslim, who attempts to buy the "perfect warrior" for his future stable of fighters. Kojiki, however, rejects the offer of easy wealth and status, choosing to walk away from the city with Kaginoko to continue their pursuit of absolute mastery. He warns his student that to truly master the world of shadows, one must be prepared to become a ghost even to those they love. As they depart into the cold night air, the transition from a boy seeking glory to a man seeking the Gates is completed, sealing the lonely fate of the warrior who will one day be known only as "Shadow".

### Chapter 7\[]

After five days of arduous travel away from the industrial mines, Kaginoko and his companions enter a desolate mountain region where the air is thick with dust and a haunting, unnatural silence. They arrive at a seemingly abandoned village identified as the home of the next Keeper, finding only a ghost town of rotting wood and overgrown paths that trigger Kaginoko's memories of childhood games played in similar forests. The eerie stillness is shattered when they encounter a group of emaciated survivors living in filth, followed by a sudden, feral ambush by a mob of "shadow-stricken" villagers who attack with mindless ferocity. Kaginoko is forced to cut through the attackers with cold efficiency, while his guide Minoro is nearly killed by a crazed woman before Kaginoko intervenes, realizing the entire settlement is a living trap designed to consume anyone seeking the fragments.

Amidst the carnage and blood-soaked streets, the group’s morale fractures as the mercenaries realize they are slaughtering possessed humans, yet Kaginoko remains singularly focused, pushing toward the village's central shrine. Under the light of a "fat" moon, he eventually encounters a young woman in a pristine yellow kimono standing calmly among the corpses—a figure who appears entirely untouched by the filth and violence. She identifies herself as a Keeper and explains that the village is actually a "prison-tomb" built by an Emperor long ago to contain those who could not be controlled, revealing that she and her kin have lived as guardians for centuries. She speaks of the Gates as a place that "people love to fear" and mocks Kaginoko's quest, suggesting that he is simply another in a long line of vain warriors seeking a legendary glory that will only lead to his own destruction.

Despite her unsettling aura, the woman does not initially attack, instead offering Kaginoko the key details in a tattered bundle, claiming that she has grown weary of the cycle of death and wishes to see what happens if the Gates are actually opened. She warns him that the fragments are "heavy" with the weight of the lives they have cost and that opening the door will lead to an end of his humanity. As she vanishes into the darkness, leaving Kaginoko with the third set of fragments, the warrior is left standing in the silence of the dead village.

### Chapter 8\[]

As winter descends and the ground hardens with frost, Kaginoko continues his secret training under Sensei, hiding his progress from Carham while waiting for his master to leave the village. Sensei eventually leads him to a high-stakes tournament in the port city of Saintai, intended as the final test of his "no ceiling" potential. In the final match, Kaginoko faces an arrogant veteran named Ronko, who mocks the boy's youth until Kaginoko uses his perfected balance to disarm him with a strike so fast it leaves the arena in silence. This victory shatters Kaginoko's remaining ties to his home; Carham witnesses the duel and is horrified by the cold, mechanical efficiency his student has adopted, leading to a final argument where Kaginoko rejects his master's pleas for humanity and follows Kojiki into the cold night.

Sensei warns Kaginoko that his legend has now attracted the attention of the capital's powerful elite, and that to master the world of shadows, he must remain a ghost to the world of light. Years of this isolated pursuit culminate as the adult Kaginoko finally stands before the colossal, mist-shrouded Gates of Shadows with the six rusted fragments in hand. Despite the visible terror of his companion Dtai and the heavy, ominous weight of the relics, Kaginoko inserts the completed key into the stone mechanism. He ignores the final warnings of the Keepers he defeated, driven by a lifelong obsession to face the demons he has prepared for since childhood.

As the massive doors groan open, a blinding white light erupts from the void, followed by a wave of sentient darkness that physically strips Kaginoko of his flesh and soul. His humanity is burned away in an instant, leaving behind only the silhouetted, two-dimensional entity known as Shadow. Standing amidst the ruins of his former self, Shadow looks back to see the spectral faces of his past—Carham, Sensei, and his lost friends—before turning his back on the world of men. He steps into the eternal darkness of the Gates, realizing that his journey was never about glory, but about becoming a living weapon capable of hunting every shadow that dwells within the void.

## Trivia\[]

- This is the first novel to have ever released for the Shadow Fight franchise.
- The novel officially reveals the real name of Shadow as Kaginoko, which was previously unknown in the main Shadow Fight games. In the games, he is universally referred to as Shadow.
  - While Kaginoko’s journey and character development are canonical, his real name is never used in the games; it is exclusive to this novel.
- The novel represents the first official narrative expansion of the Shadow Fight universe outside of the games.
- The story in the novel is set prior to the events of Shadow Fight 2, providing additional backstory and context for the character’s transformation.
- Novel uses _non-linear narrative_ where odd chapters follow the adult Kaginoko’s quest for the seals, while even chapters serve as flashbacks to his childhood training under mentors to explain the origins of his techniques.
- The book’s back cover features promotional quotes from Russian book bloggers.
